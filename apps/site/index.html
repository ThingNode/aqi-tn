The corridors

Measured where the walk to school happens.

The Colombo district puts Sri Lanka's heaviest traffic corridors and its densest school populations on the same streets. Almost none of that exposure is measured at the point where a child meets it.

  • Fifteen corridor stations funded and scheduled for deployment
  • Fifteen more on the same five highest-emission corridors
  • Evidence aimed at the vehicle emission standards, Gazette 1738/37

The network

Forty-five stations,
one open instrument.

Thirty new stations join the fifteen already funded — fifteen more on the corridors, fifteen inside school grounds. Every site runs the same open-hardware monitor, so a reading from one is comparable to a reading from any other.

For schools

Fifteen of them go
inside the gate.

Playground and classroom air, not only the road outside. Siting runs through Magicbit, Thingsnode's sister company, already working with these schools on STEM education — so what is missing is the instrument, not the relationship.

  • An alert to the school when levels cross WHO guidance
  • Read alongside partner doctors and health officials
  • No cost to the school, during the grant and after it

Guidance is population-level and not medical advice.

The question underneath

Does the corridor outside change the child inside?

A cross-referencing engine reads corridor and classroom pollutant data against paediatric respiratory admissions, and returns a risk index specific to one school and the road beside it. As far as we can establish, no study in South Asia has joined corridor and classroom exposure to children's respiratory outcomes at this scale.

The admissions side is aggregate and anonymised: no record about any child, ever.
